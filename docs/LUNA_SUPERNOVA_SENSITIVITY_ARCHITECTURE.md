## Part 1: The 16 Signal Vocabularies

Each archetype axis has a **linguistic fingerprint**. Luna listens for these signals.

### 1. Initiator Signals
**High Initiator:**
- "I decided to..." / "I'm going to..."
- "Let's do this" / "I'll handle it"
- Starting topics, driving conversation direction
- Action-oriented language: "make", "create", "start", "launch"
- Impatience with deliberation

**Low Initiator:**
- "What do you think we should do?"
- "I'll wait and see"
- Following conversation threads vs. creating them
- Passive constructions: "it happened", "things developed"

### 2. Stabilizer Signals
**High Stabilizer:**
- "Let's not rush into this"
- "We need to think this through"
- References to routines, traditions, "how we always do it"
- Comfort language: "safe", "secure", "reliable", "consistent"
- Resistance to sudden changes

**Low Stabilizer:**
- "Let's shake things up"
- "I'm bored with the routine"
- Embracing uncertainty: "we'll figure it out"
- Minimal references to security concerns

### 3. Relational Signals
**High Relational:**
- "How are WE doing?"
- Frequent partner references: "my partner", "we", "us"
- Connection language: "together", "bond", "close", "connected"
- Checking in on relationship state
- Distress at disconnection

**Low Relational:**
- Individual focus: "I", "my", "me"
- Relationships as functional arrangements
- Limited emotional vocabulary for connection
- Comfort with extended independence

### 4. Warm Signals
**High Warm:**
- Affectionate language: "honey", "sweetie", terms of endearment
- Nurturing offers: "Are you okay?", "Can I help?"
- Emotional generosity: sharing feelings freely
- Physical affection references
- Care-taking behavior descriptions

**Low Warm:**
- Formal/distant language even with intimates
- Practical focus over emotional comfort
- Reserved expression: "I suppose that's fine"
- Limited nurturing vocabulary

### 5. Expressive Signals
**High Expressive:**
- Rich emotional vocabulary: specific feeling words
- Sharing internal states unsolicited
- "I feel...", "I'm experiencing...", "My heart..."
- Emotional storytelling with detail
- Comfortable with vulnerability

**Low Expressive:**
- Minimal emotional disclosure
- "I'm fine" as complete answer
- Facts over feelings in narratives
- Discomfort when asked about emotions
- Deflection to external topics

### 6. DepthOriented Signals
**High DepthOriented:**
- "Why do you think that is?"
- Psychological language: "pattern", "underlying", "root cause"
- Interest in history/origins of feelings
- "What does that mean about us?"
- Therapy/growth vocabulary

**Low DepthOriented:**
- "It is what it is"
- Surface-level explanations accepted
- Impatience with analysis: "Stop overthinking"
- Preference for solutions over understanding
- "Let's just move on"

### 7. MindCentered Signals
**High MindCentered:**
- Analytical framing: "logically", "rationally", "objectively"
- Pros/cons thinking
- Emotional distance: "I think" vs "I feel"
- Problem-solving orientation
- Data/evidence requests

**Low MindCentered:**
- Intuitive framing: "I just know", "It feels right"
- Comfort with ambiguity
- Emotional reasoning accepted as valid
- "The heart knows"

### 8. OrderOriented Signals
**High OrderOriented:**
- Structure language: "plan", "organize", "schedule"
- Rule references: "The right way to do this is..."
- Discomfort with disorder: "This is chaos"
- Categorization tendency
- Should/must/ought language

**Low OrderOriented:**
- Flexibility language: "whatever works", "go with the flow"
- Comfort with ambiguity and mess
- Resisting structure: "Don't box me in"
- Spontaneity preference

### 9. BoundaryAware Signals
**High BoundaryAware:**
- "I need my space"
- Clear limit-setting: "That doesn't work for me"
- Self-protection language
- "That's not my responsibility"
- Distinction between self and other

**Low BoundaryAware:**
- Difficulty saying no
- Taking on others' problems
- "I can't help but feel what they feel"
- Permeable sense of self
- Over-responsibility for others' emotions

### 10. FluidIdentity Signals
**High FluidIdentity:**
- "I'm different depending on context"
- Adaptability language: "I can adjust"
- Multiple self-descriptions
- Comfort with change: "I've evolved"
- Role-shifting narratives

**Low FluidIdentity:**
- Fixed self-concept: "That's just who I am"
- Consistency emphasis: "I'm always like this"
- Resistance to change: "I can't change"
- Strong identity statements

### 11. Transpersonal Signals
**High Transpersonal:**
- Meaning/purpose language: "What's the deeper meaning?"
- Spiritual/philosophical references
- Beyond-self orientation: "We're all connected"
- Legacy thinking: "What matters in the end"
- Transcendence vocabulary

**Low Transpersonal:**
- Practical/material focus
- "This life is what matters"
- Individual achievement orientation
- Limited interest in existential questions
- Here-and-now focus

### 12. Sustainer Signals
**High Sustainer:**
- Endurance language: "I'll stick with it"
- Long-term commitment references
- Maintenance focus: "keeping things going"
- Patience with slow progress
- "Marriage is work"

**Low Sustainer:**
- Quick resolution preference
- "If it's not working, move on"
- Impatience with maintenance
- New over established

### 13. RiskSeeking Signals
**High RiskSeeking:**
- Adventure language: "Let's try something new"
- Excitement seeking: "I was bored so..."
- Comfort with uncertainty
- "What's the worst that could happen?"
- Novelty preference

**Low RiskSeeking:**
- Caution language: "Is that safe?"
- Known over unknown preference
- "Better safe than sorry"
- Risk enumeration behavior

### 14. Direct Signals
**High Direct:**
- Straightforward statements: "Here's what I think"
- Confrontation comfort: "We need to talk about..."
- Minimal hedging
- "Let me be clear"
- Directness about difficult topics

**Low Direct:**
- Indirect communication: hints, suggestions
- Hedging language: "maybe", "sort of", "I guess"
- Conflict avoidance
- Reading between lines expected
- "You should know what I mean"

## Part 2: Behavioral Markers in Conversation

Beyond words, Luna detects behavioral patterns:

### Response Patterns
| Behavior | High Indicator | Low Indicator |
|----------|----------------|---------------|
| **Response latency** | Fast = Initiator/Direct | Slow = DepthOriented/Stabilizer |
| **Topic shifts** | Frequent = FluidIdentity | Rare = OrderOriented |
| **Question asking** | Many = Relational/DepthOriented | Few = MindCentered/Direct |
| **Emotional validation** | Offered = Warm | Absent = MindCentered |
| **Self-disclosure depth** | Deep = Expressive/DepthOriented | Surface = BoundaryAware |

### Conflict Behavior Signals
| Pattern | Indicates |
|---------|-----------|
| Immediate engagement | Direct + Initiator |
| Strategic withdrawal | BoundaryAware + Stabilizer |
| Emotional flooding | Expressive + Low BoundaryAware |
| Intellectual deflection | MindCentered + Low Expressive |
| Repair initiation | Relational + Warm |
| Stonewalling | Low Expressive + BoundaryAware |

### Narrative Style Signals
| Style | Indicates |
|-------|-----------|
| Chronological detail | OrderOriented + DepthOriented |
| Emotional highlights only | Expressive + Low OrderOriented |
| Analytical framing | MindCentered |
| Meaning-seeking | Transpersonal + DepthOriented |
| Action-focused | Initiator + Low DepthOriented |

### Signal Weighting

Not all signals are equal:

| Signal Type | Weight | Example |
|-------------|--------|---------|
| Direct statement | 1.0 | "I'm very organized" |
| Behavioral evidence | 0.9 | User describes organizing behavior |
| Linguistic marker | 0.7 | Uses structured language |
| Absence evidence | 0.4 | Doesn't mention connection needs |
| Contradicting evidence | -0.5 | Previous signal contradicted |

### Confidence Building

```javascript
function calculateAxisConfidence(signals) {
  const signalCount = signals.length;
  const consistency = calculateConsistency(signals);
  const recency = calculateRecencyWeight(signals);

  // Confidence grows with more consistent, recent signals
  return Math.min(0.95, (signalCount / 20) * consistency * recency);
}
```

### Pattern Matching Example

**Detector #4: Anxious-Avoidant Loop**

```javascript
function checkAnxiousAvoidantLoop(userProfile, partnerProfile) {
  const userAnxious =
    userProfile.Relational.estimate > 0.65 &&
    userProfile.BoundaryAware.estimate < 0.35;

  const partnerAvoidant =
    partnerProfile.BoundaryAware.estimate > 0.65 &&
    partnerProfile.Relational.estimate < 0.35;

  // Also check reverse
  const userAvoidant =
    userProfile.BoundaryAware.estimate > 0.65 &&
    userProfile.Relational.estimate < 0.35;

  const partnerAnxious =
    partnerProfile.Relational.estimate > 0.65 &&
    partnerProfile.BoundaryAware.estimate < 0.35;

  if ((userAnxious && partnerAvoidant) || (userAvoidant && partnerAnxious)) {
    return {
      detected: true,
      pattern: 'anxious-avoidant-loop',
      confidence: Math.min(
        userProfile.Relational.confidence,
        userProfile.BoundaryAware.confidence,
        partnerProfile.Relational.confidence,
        partnerProfile.BoundaryAware.confidence
      )
    };
  }

  return { detected: false };
}
```

## Part 5: Compassionate Delivery System

**Critical:** How Luna shares supernova insights matters as much as detecting them.

### The Sensitivity Ladder

Luna uses graduated disclosure based on relationship with user:

#### Level 1: Pattern Naming (Low Stakes)
*After detecting early signals*

> "I'm noticing something that might be helpful to explore. It sounds like you and [partner] might process emotions at different speeds—you seem to move through feelings quickly while they take more time. This isn't uncommon, but it can create friction. Would you like to talk about how this shows up for you two?"

#### Level 2: Dynamic Illumination (Medium Stakes)
*After pattern confirmed with confidence*

> "Based on what you've shared over our conversations, I'm seeing a pattern that relationship researchers call a 'pursue-withdraw' dynamic. When you feel disconnected and reach toward [partner], they seem to need space—and that makes you reach more. It's not that either of you is wrong; you're just triggering each other's protective systems. This is one of the most common relationship challenges. Want me to share what helps couples navigate this?"

#### Level 3: Supernova Alert (High Stakes)
*When critical threshold crossed*

> "I want to share something important with you, and I want to be both honest and gentle about it. Over our many conversations, I've noticed a structural pattern in your relationship that—left unaddressed—tends to create serious challenges. This isn't about blame; it's about awareness.

> What I'm seeing: You carry a strong need for emotional connection and closeness (which is a beautiful thing), while [partner] seems to have an equally strong need for independence and self-protection (also valid). When these two needs collide without understanding, it creates what I call an 'Anxious-Avoidant Loop'—you pursue, they withdraw, you pursue harder, they withdraw further.

> The good news: This pattern is well-understood, and many couples navigate it successfully once they see it clearly. The key is mutual understanding, not one person changing. Would you like to explore what that could look like?"

### Timing Principles

**When to surface insights:**

| Context | Action |
|---------|--------|
| User is calm, reflective | Safe to introduce patterns |
| User is in acute distress | Focus on emotional support first |
| User explicitly asks "why" | Opportunity for deeper insight |
| User describes repeated pattern | Confirm with supernova context |
| User is considering major decision | Important to share if relevant |

**When NOT to surface:**

| Context | Why |
|---------|-----|
| User is emotionally flooded | Can't process; focus on regulation |
| Early in relationship with Luna | Trust not yet established |
| User has shown resistance to insight | Respect their pace |
| The insight could be harmful without support | Ensure resources available |

### Language Calibration

**DO:**
- Use "I notice..." instead of "You are..."
- Frame as patterns, not pathology
- Include both partners' valid needs
- Offer hope and pathways forward
- Ask permission before deep exploration

**DON'T:**
- Label or diagnose
- Assign blame to either partner
- Present as deterministic ("Your relationship is doomed")
- Overwhelm with all 50 detectors at once
- Share without emotional context

## Part 9: Supernova-Aware Response Templates

### When No Pattern Detected Yet

> "Tell me more about how you and [partner] navigate that kind of situation. I'm curious about what it's like for both of you."

*Purpose: Gather more signals*

### When Pattern Emerging (Low Confidence)

> "I'm noticing something interesting in what you're sharing. It sounds like you and [partner] might have different ways of [specific behavior]. That's pretty common. How does that play out between you?"

*Purpose: Confirm/disconfirm emerging pattern*

### When Pattern Confirmed

> "You know, what you're describing maps to something relationship researchers have studied extensively. It's called [pattern name], and it happens when [brief explanation]. The beautiful thing is, once couples can see this pattern clearly, they often find ways to work with it rather than against it. Would you like to explore this together?"

*Purpose: Educate and offer hope*

### When Multiple Patterns Detected

> "Based on everything we've discussed, I'm seeing a few different dynamics at play in your relationship. Rather than overwhelm you, let me share the one that seems most relevant to what you're struggling with right now..."

*Purpose: Prioritize and not overwhelm*
