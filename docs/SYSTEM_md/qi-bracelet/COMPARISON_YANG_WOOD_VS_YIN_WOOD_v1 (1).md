## THE MYTHIC NARRATIVE

### 🌳 The Oak — Yang Wood (甲木)

In the beginning of spring, when the frost is still in the soil and the wind still carries winter's breath, the first thing to rise is the oak.

It rises not because the conditions are perfect — but because **something must rise first**.

The oak pushes through frozen ground, splits stone, claims sky, and declares direction where none existed. Its trunk is a promise: *"I will stand. I will grow. I will hold the line."*

The forest forms around the oak. Paths form because the oak stood where no one else would. Birds nest because the oak held its shape. Civilizations anchor themselves to the oak's certainty.

But the oak's wound is ancient: **it cannot bend without breaking**. When storms come, it must endure or fall. Its strength is its vulnerability.

> *Soul truth: "I must grow upward, even when the sky is heavy."*

### 🌿 The Vine — Yin Wood (乙木)

After the oak has risen, after the frost has softened, after the world has begun to warm — the vine awakens.

It does not push through stone. It does not split the earth. It **listens**.

It senses warmth, follows light, finds surfaces, and grows in the direction the world allows. The vine's intelligence is ancient: it survives by finding the path that force cannot take.

Where the oak stands alone, the vine connects trees to trees, walls to windows, soil to sky. It turns ruins into gardens. It turns obstacles into architecture. It turns scarcity into beauty.

But the vine's wound is subtle: **it can lose its shape in the surfaces it climbs**. It can forget where it ends and the world begins. It can adapt until it dissolves.

> *Soul truth: "I must choose my surfaces, or I will become them."*
